JUBILATION (3) — CRITICAL ISSUES AND IMPORTANT FIXES
========================================================

Purpose
-------
This document consolidates the issues that should be fixed in the current version before publication/submission.

1. CRITICAL — should be fixed before submission.
2. MAJOR — important for scientific validity/reproducibility.
3. IMPORTANT — wording, interpretation, or documentation improvements.
4. ALREADY FIXED — improvements from earlier versions that should be preserved.

Priority is technical correctness first, then reproducibility, then wording.


========================================================
1. CRITICAL ISSUES
========================================================

CRITICAL 1 — Current 2026 pension value: €42.15 vs €42.52
---------------------------------------------------------

The manuscript currently uses:

    AR_2026 = €42.15

For the pension value applicable from 1 July 2026, the official value is €42.52.

Therefore, if the paper claims to use the current 2026 statutory framework:

    AR_2026 = €42.52

and the 45-EP Standardrentner gross pension becomes:

    45 × €42.52 = €1,913.40/month

instead of:

    45 × €42.15 = €1,896.75/month

ACTION:
- Decide whether the model is a pre-July-2026 or post-July-2026 model.
- If current 2026: replace €42.15 with €42.52.
- Recalculate the Standardrentner validation and every dependent result.


CRITICAL 2 — Standardrentner net-pension contradiction
--------------------------------------------------------

The paper contains incompatible results.

Table I gives approximately:

45 EP
→ Gross pension: €1,896.75

Parent with children:
KV = €165.97
PV = €68.28
Net before tax = €1,662.50

Childless:
KV = €165.97
PV = €79.66
Net before tax = €1,651.12

But another section says:

"Standardrentner ... yields exactly €1,675.25 net per month."

These cannot all describe the same calculation.

ACTION:
Create one fully reproducible chain:

45 EP
→ pension value
→ gross pension
→ KV contribution
→ Zusatzbeitrag
→ Pflegeversicherung
→ tax
→ final net pension

Then remove every conflicting value.

Clearly define:
- gross pension;
- net after social contributions;
- taxable pension;
- final disposable/net pension.

Do not use "net pension" unless the exact deductions are defined.


CRITICAL 3 — Pension indexation to 2070 must follow the correct statutory mechanism
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The manuscript now includes future pension-value projection, which is good.

However, implementation must be checked against the exact structure of §68 SGB VI.

Document:
- wage series;
- contribution-rate assumptions;
- pensioner quotient;
- sustainability factor;
- relevant statutory parameters;
- treatment of future reforms.

If implementation is simplified, do NOT call it an exact implementation.

Recommended wording if simplified:

"The projection follows the structure of the statutory pension-indexation mechanism
under §68 SGB VI using explicitly stated assumptions for future statutory parameters."

If the code implements the exact formula, document every component.


CRITICAL 4 — 2026–2031 special pension-level rules need to be considered
-----------------------------------------------------------------------

The model appears to apply the normal pension-indexation mechanism over the whole forecast horizon.

The German statutory framework contains special provisions affecting pension-value
determination through 2031.

ACTION:
- Verify whether the implementation includes the relevant transitional/special rules.
- If yes, document them.
- If no, add them or explicitly define the model as a simplified scenario.

Distinguish:

2026–2031:
special statutory/transitional treatment where applicable

2032 onward:
baseline statutory indexation framework, conditional on policy assumptions.


CRITICAL 5 — Grundrentenzuschlag (§76g SGB VI) is too simplified
---------------------------------------------------------------

The manuscript appears to treat the Grundrentenzuschlag as a relatively simple
additional EP component.

The actual statutory mechanism is more complicated and depends on:
- Grundrentenzeiten;
- Grundrentenbewertungszeiten;
- average EP thresholds;
- statutory valuation;
- maximum credited months/years;
- statutory factors and caps.

ACTION:
Either:
A) implement the full statutory §76g calculation, or
B) explicitly label the current approach as a simplified approximation.

Recommended wording if simplified:

"Grundrenteneffekte are approximated using a simplified eligibility and benefit rule
rather than a full implementation of §76g SGB VI."

Do not call a simplified version an exact statutory implementation.


CRITICAL 6 — Annuity-due formula appears mathematically mislabeled
---------------------------------------------------------------

The paper defines an annuity factor as:

    ä_(K|rpost)
    =
    [1 - (1+r/12)^(-12K)] / (r/12)

This is the ordinary-annuity present-value factor.

For a true annuity-due:

    ä_n|i
    =
    (1+i) × [1-(1+i)^(-n)] / i

where:

    i = r/12

ACTION:
Either:
- change "annuity due" to "ordinary annuity", or
- use the correct annuity-due equation.

Then recalculate all required monthly savings results because this can affect the
reported €35–€220/month range.


CRITICAL 7 — Timing convention for savings and withdrawals is missing
---------------------------------------------------------------------

Specify:
- contributions at beginning or end of month;
- withdrawals at beginning or end of month;
- exact retirement start;
- timing of final contribution;
- whether savings/withdrawals are constant in real euros.

Add the actual convention used in code.


CRITICAL 8 — Statutory pension vs total retirement income must be separated
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

Several tables use "pension" while NRR is based on total retirement income.

Use separate columns:

Cohort
Pre-Retirement Net Earnings
Statutory Pension
Private Retirement Income
Total Retirement Income
NRR
Below Adequacy Benchmark

Do this in both the main results and sensitivity tables.


CRITICAL 9 — NRR must be defined as median of individual ratios
---------------------------------------------------------------

In general:

    median(Y / X) != median(Y) / median(X)

State explicitly:

"Individual-level NRR is calculated for each simulated person as total net retirement
income divided by pre-retirement net earnings. Cohort NRR is reported as the median
of individual-level NRRs."

Then verify the code follows this definition.

2. MAJOR SCIENTIFIC / METHODOLOGICAL ISSUES
========================================================

MAJOR 1 — Deskilling coefficient should not be described as a causal "penalty"
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The model reports approximately:

    beta = -1.0632

which corresponds to:

    exp(-1.0632)-1 ≈ -65.5%

The arithmetic is correct.

However, observational OLS does not automatically establish a causal wage penalty.

Recommended:

"Qualification deskilling is associated with an estimated 65.5% lower conditional wage."

If causal interpretation is intended, provide a credible identification strategy.


MAJOR 2 — "Occupational sorting" needs to be supported by the regression
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Check whether occupation and/or industry are actually included.

If included:
- show them in the model specification/table.

If not:
- remove "occupational sorting" from the interpretation.

Potential controls:
- occupation;
- industry;
- region;
- full-time/part-time;
- firm characteristics.


MAJOR 3 — Credential-recognition policy should be framed as a counterfactual
----------------------------------------------------------------------------

The OLS regression does not establish that credential recognition causes the wage
difference.

Recommended:

"Accelerated credential recognition could reduce the wage disadvantage associated
with qualification mismatch; however, the present observational specification does
not identify a causal treatment effect."

Clearly distinguish:
- empirical association;
- modeled counterfactual;
- policy interpretation.


MAJOR 4 — Wealth regression needs full reproducibility information
-------------------------------------------------------------------

Provide:
- sample size;
- dependent-variable unit;
- wealth definition;
- individual vs household wealth;
- housing wealth inclusion;
- debt treatment;
- all covariates;
- coefficients;
- standard errors;
- p-values;
- R²;
- sample restrictions.

Add a complete regression table or appendix.


MAJOR 5 — IHS transformation must define theta and units
----------------------------------------------------------

The paper uses:

    IHS(W) = ln(theta W + sqrt((theta W)^2 + 1))

State the exact theta.

Also state whether W is measured in euros, thousands of euros, or another scale.


MAJOR 6 — Vine Copula needs model-selection documentation
-----------------------------------------------------------

Provide:
- vine structure/type;
- pair-copula families;
- estimated parameters;
- selection criterion;
- AIC/BIC;
- goodness-of-fit diagnostics.

Recommended appendix:

Edge | Copula Family | Parameter(s) | AIC | BIC

Also compare observed vs synthetic:
- Pearson correlations;
- Spearman correlations;
- quantiles;
- tail dependence where relevant.


MAJOR 7 — "Moment-preserving" should be softened unless fully demonstrated
-------------------------------------------------------------------------

The validation table is a major improvement.

Safer wording:

"closely reproduces selected empirical moments"

rather than "moment-preserving", unless preservation is demonstrated across the intended
distribution.

Validate:
- mean;
- SD;
- p10;
- p25;
- p50;
- p75;
- p90;
- cross-variable correlations;
- subgroup distributions.


MAJOR 8 — Explain why the sample sizes differ
----------------------------------------------

The paper has approximately:

50,000 synthetic population
40,500 lifecycle simulation
37,210 wage regression

Explain:
- exclusions;
- missing data;
- age restrictions;
- eligibility restrictions;
- whether samples are subsets of the same generated population;
- whether different random draws are used.


MAJOR 9 — 52.1% below benchmark needs precise uncertainty terminology
------------------------------------------------------------------------

The paper reports:

52.1%
with sensitivity range:
44.6%–58.2%

This is good.

If the range comes from changing assumptions/scenarios, call it:

"scenario/sensitivity range"

not "95% confidence interval."


MAJOR 10 — Explain exactly what the 78% policy result represents
-----------------------------------------------------------------

Specify whether 78% means:
- percentage crossing €1,113;
- percentage no longer below the benchmark;
- percentage above a replacement-rate target;
- percentage no longer requiring means-tested support;
- something else.

Provide the exact numerator and denominator.

3. IMPORTANT INTERPRETATION / MODELING ISSUES
========================================================

IMPORTANT 1 — Clarify the €1,113 benchmark
-------------------------------------------

The paper defines:

    €563 + €550 = €1,113

This is acceptable as an illustrative model benchmark.

€563 is the 2026 single-adult Regelbedarf.
€550 is an assumed housing cost.

Therefore €1,113 is NOT:
- a universal national poverty line;
- a universal Grundsicherung entitlement threshold;
- a universal housing amount.

Recommended wording:

"illustrative single-adult retirement adequacy benchmark, defined as the 2026
single-adult Regelbedarf plus an assumed €550/month housing cost."


IMPORTANT 2 — SGB XII €10,000 asset assumption needs qualification
-------------------------------------------------------------------

Do not present €10,000 as the complete legal asset test.

Recommended:

"The baseline model applies a €10,000 protected liquid-financial-asset assumption
within the SGB XII means-test module while separately modeling statutory exemptions."

Distinguish:
- liquid financial assets;
- protected retirement provisions;
- owner-occupied housing;
- other protected assets;
- income eligibility.


IMPORTANT 3 — Verify constant-2026-euro treatment everywhere
------------------------------------------------------------

Verify that constant-2026 euros are used consistently for:
- wages;
- pensions;
- wealth;
- housing;
- benchmark;
- portfolio returns;
- savings targets.

If real euros are used, specify:
- deflator/CPI;
- base year;
- nominal-to-real conversion procedure.


IMPORTANT 4 — Portfolio returns need complete definition
----------------------------------------------------------

Baseline/sensitivity:
- 2.0%;
- 4.5%;
- 6.0%.

Define whether returns are:
- nominal or real;
- before or after fees;
- before or after taxes;
- arithmetic or geometric;
- deterministic or stochastic.


IMPORTANT 5 — BTarget €1,800 cap needs justification
---------------------------------------------------

The model defines:

    BTarget =
    max(€1,113, min(€1,800, 0.60 × w_net))

Explain the origin of €1,800.

If normative/model-assumed, label it clearly.
If empirical, provide the source.


IMPORTANT 6 — Gender interpretation needs caution
-------------------------------------------------

The gender result can be consistent with differences in:
- earnings;
- employment duration;
- labor-force participation;
- caregiving;
- longevity.

But the regression alone does not necessarily identify each mechanism.

If possible, add a decomposition of the gender pension gap.


IMPORTANT 7 — Domestic-experience coefficient needs a precise variable definition
---------------------------------------------------------------------------------

If:

    beta = 0.0363

then approximately:

    exp(0.0363)-1 = 3.7%

But because nonlinear experience terms are included, do not describe this as a constant
3.7% return for every year.

Define "Domestic Experience" precisely:
- years since arrival;
- years in Germany;
- years employed in Germany;
- years of German labor-market experience.


IMPORTANT 8 — Native median pension comparison needs population matching
------------------------------------------------------------------------

If the model claims to match a DRV long-term-insured median, verify identical:
- population;
- age;
- insurance history;
- pension definition;
- gross/net status;
- reference year.

"German native" is not automatically equivalent to "DRV long-term insured."


IMPORTANT 9 — Ukrainian calibration needs exact date/source
------------------------------------------------------------

For statements such as "over 1.1 million", specify the reference date.

For IAB/BAMF/SOEP calibration:
- exact survey wave;
- survey year;
- population definition;
- weighting;
- age restrictions.


IMPORTANT 10 — Foreign pension entitlements need clearer treatment
-------------------------------------------------------------------

State whether Ukrainian migrants/refugees have:
- zero foreign pension;
- excluded foreign pension;
- modeled foreign pension;
- potentially portable foreign pension rights.

If excluded:

"Reported retirement income represents German retirement income and excludes
potentially portable foreign pension entitlements."


IMPORTANT 11 — Institutional-policy uncertainty should be worded correctly
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

Avoid calling the whole institutional environment "stationary."

Better:

"The model applies currently legislated institutional rules and statutory indexation
mechanisms but does not anticipate future discretionary legislative reforms."


IMPORTANT 12 — Add mortality/health-state assumptions
------------------------------------------------------

State whether the model includes:
- mortality;
- disability;
- long-term care;
- unemployment;
- health-related labor-force exits.

If absent, explicitly say so.


IMPORTANT 13 — Household composition should be a limitation
------------------------------------------------------------

Retirement adequacy and Grundsicherung depend on:
- single/couple status;
- partner income;
- children;
- caregiving;
- housing arrangement.

If the model is individual-level, explicitly state whether household pooling is excluded.


IMPORTANT 14 — Housing should be a major sensitivity dimension
----------------------------------------------------------------

Because €550 is an assumed housing cost, test a defensible range such as:
- €450;
- €550;
- €700;
- €850.

Document whether housing is rent-only or includes utilities/heating.


IMPORTANT 15 — Employment trajectory should be added to sensitivity analysis
----------------------------------------------------------------------------

Add:
- low employment;
- baseline;
- high employment.

Employment duration may materially affect migrant/refugee pension adequacy.

========================================================
9. FINAL ASSESSMENT
========================================================

The current version is significantly stronger than the earlier versions.

The conceptual architecture is credible and the manuscript now has:
- a clearer microsimulation framework;
- improved statutory pension mechanics;
- better validation;
- sensitivity analysis;
- clearer limitations;
- more cautious interpretation.

The remaining weaknesses are concentrated in technical implementation and
reproducibility rather than overall research design.

The most dangerous issues are:

A. current 2026 pension value;
B. contradictory Standardrentner net calculation;
C. exact pension-indexation implementation;
D. Grundrente implementation;
E. annuity-due formula;
F. distinction between statutory pension and total retirement income;
G. definition of NRR.

If these are corrected and all dependent calculations are regenerated, the manuscript
will be substantially more defensible.

Recommended status after these fixes:

"Technically promising; ready for final methodological and empirical validation,
but not yet ready for submission."
